1

Qualify

30 minDesk

Kills bad jobs before anyone drives out. Four checks, all of which can disqualify a site outright.

  • Airspace. Much of Carlsbad sits under McClellan-Palomar (CRQ) controlled airspace — LAANC per flight, with a ceiling that may be lower than you need. Check per address; never assume.
  • Ground risk. Overspray reaches further than the aircraft does, so "not over people" is stricter here than Part 107 alone requires.
  • Water in and out. A spigot and a hose route — and a containment plan, because detergent reaching a storm drain is a regulated discharge in California.
  • Pre-existing damage. Photograph it. Damage you didn't document is damage you caused.
Gate — airspace clear · water solved · owner consent in writing
2

Scout

45–60 min on siteRPIC · Autel 4T V2

Two automated passes. A nadir grid at 70–80% forward overlap builds the roof and massing; oblique orbits at ~45° build the façades. Skip the obliques and the walls — half your billable area — reconstruct as mush.

RGB and thermal are captured co-registered on one gimbal. That is the single biggest reason for this airframe: it turns cross-sensor alignment from a per-frame problem into a fixed constant.

The weather window is narrower than you think

The grime proxy reads evaporative cooling — damp surfaces read cooler than the dry surface around them. That only works on dry, sun-loaded surfaces. Carlsbad's morning marine layer flattens thermal contrast completely.

Practical window: 10:00–15:00, no rain in the preceding 24 h. A scan in the marine layer is not a degraded scan — it is a useless one. Reschedule.

Gate — in-window conditions · coverage complete · exclusions noted on site
3

Process

1–3 h computeUnattended

RGB builds the geometry; thermal supplies the values. The order is not negotiable — thermal can't feature-match, because its landmarks move with temperature.

Photogrammetry gives you a thermal-textured model. It does not give you view-angle-weighted, reflection-rejected per-face temperature with a confidence score. That is our stage, and it is what every downstream decision is made from.

This latency shapes the whole business

You cannot scan at 09:00 and wash at 09:30. The answer is scan day / wash day: batch 4–6 properties in one scouting morning, process overnight, wash them across the following days.

That isn't a workaround, it's a better business. It separates a cheap, low-risk, high-margin scouting operation from an expensive, liability-heavy wash operation — and the twin becomes a sales asset you show before the customer commits.

4

Prescribe & review

10–20 minHuman · mandatory

The agent prescribes, the deterministic safety layer gates, and then a person signs. What the reviewer is actually checking:

  • Nothing that should be excluded was missed. The classifier catches HVAC and roof protrusions. It will not catch a koi pond.
  • Solar reads DI-water-only, under the ceiling, in every phase. Detergent residue cuts panel output; pressure cracks cells.
  • Grime scores agree with the photograph. A zone reading 60% that looks clean means the thermal was confounded — shade, a wet patch, a reflection.
  • Low-confidence classifications get human eyes before dispatch.
Gate — reviewer signature, written to the audit log as waiver & liability evidence

Two windows that don't overlap

Scanning wants surfaces hot and dry (10:00–15:00). Solar panels must not be washed hot — cold water on sun-loaded glass is a thermal-shock risk, so panels want early morning or evening. The two windows are mutually exclusive, which is one more reason scan day and wash day are different days.

Zones run solar-first and top-down, so runoff never re-dirties finished work. Each zone gets pre-soak → chemical → dwell → rinse; pre-soak and rinse are water-only by construction. Throughout, the operator stays in command — the software reduces the skill required, never the authority.

6

Verify

15 min + dry-downCrew

The dry-down problem

The grime proxy reads wet as dirty. Run a verification thermal scan straight after washing and it reports the entire building as maximally soiled. Worse — a drying surface dries unevenly, so you get a confident, detailed, completely spurious grime map.

This isn't a bug. It's a physical limit of inferring soiling from evaporative cooling, and it lands squarely on the verification half of the closed loop.

Run the thermal re-scan anyway — as a research capture, to build the dry-down curve. But don't market thermal verification until that curve exists.

7

Close

15 minMostly automated

Customer report — before/after twin, per-zone results, what was excluded and why. Invoice. Book the next service, whose pre-scan becomes this job's rigorous verification.

Then the part that compounds: the deviation log. Prescribed versus actual pressure, dwell, standoff, outcome. The surface table ships as unvalidated defaults and only becomes real numbers by accumulating these deltas across jobs. Treat the log as the product, not as telemetry exhaust.
